## Script

### intro

Sir. Field report number four in the cryptocurrency file. Today's subject: the price. Why is this thing worth anything at all? A line in a ledger. Nothing you can touch, no government behind it. And a single one of them reached a hundred and twenty-six thousand dollars. The earlier reports covered how it works and how it is held. That leaves the question the humans themselves fight about. I will report where that price comes from, and why it shakes the way it does. <short pause> And how, in a single day, more than one and a half million humans lost everything they had put in... while the price itself only dipped.

### value

The first thing I looked for: what is behind this coin? Gold in a vault? No. A government guarantee? No. A company paying dividends? No. Behind it are two things: it's scarce... and people believe it has value. Before Command laughs: that's also what's behind their paper money since 1971. <short pause> But the paper has a government that collects taxes in it. This one has... faith. They compare it to gold. Most of gold's value is not about what you can make with it. It is rare, and everyone agrees it is precious. So they call bitcoin digital gold. Twenty-one million, and no new mine will ever be discovered. Some of them say its price is the cost of the electricity burned to make it. Wrong. <short pause> If you spend a million dollars digging a hole, the hole is not worth a million dollars. For scale: all the bitcoin on the planet, at its peak, was worth about two and a half trillion dollars. All the gold is worth more than ten times that. So who is buying? First: the speculator. Buying to sell it for more later. That is most of them. Second: the escaper. Living where the currency is collapsing, wanting anything else. Third: someone who wants to move money outside the banks. And fourth: institutions. Funds and companies. They arrived late. And the more people accept it, the more it is worth. Like the telephone. The first human to buy a telephone... had nobody to call. So nobody sets the price. The price is simply the last number two humans agreed on: one who wanted to sell, and one who wanted to buy. <short pause> Nothing more than that.

### history

Then I looked at the history of the price. 2011: from one dollar to thirty dollars... and back down to two. 2013: eleven hundred. Down to two hundred. 2017: twenty thousand. Down to three thousand. 2021: sixty-nine thousand. Down to fifteen. And 2025: a hundred and twenty-six thousand... down to under sixty. Five times up to the sky. Five times down by half or more. <short pause> Surprised every time. To be fair: each crash has been gentler than the last. The first one was ninety-three percent. The latest, fifty-four. <short pause> Maybe it is growing up. Maybe not. And every bubble had its story. 2013: people running from the banks of Cyprus. 2017: anyone launching a coin and raising millions. 2021: governments printing money during the pandemic, and everyone stuck at home. And 2025: Wall Street walked in. The cycle never changes. The price rises, the papers write, your dentist says buy, everybody gets in... and down it goes. <short pause> Four years later, again. Short memories. Someone who bought at the top in 2017 waited three years just to get their money back. Someone who bought at the top in 2021: two and a half years. The bank of the central banks studied seven years of data: most people who came in through the apps... lost money. Because three quarters of them downloaded the app when the price was already high. And every time it falls, their press announces its death. They have announced it more than four hundred times. It is still breathing. The humans have noticed a pattern: every peak came a year to a year and a half after a halving. A smaller reward, so fewer new coins... and the price takes off. Is that a law? No. It has happened four times. <short pause> Humans have built religions on less.

### shake

Why does it swing so hard? One: the market is small next to the planet's money. One big seller... and the whole price moves. Reason two: there is no anchor. A share in a company has profits behind it. A house has rent. This has expectations. Three: the market never closes. Nobody shuts it when it loses its mind. Four: the news. One rich man's post sends it up. One government decision sends it down. It's meant to be a safe harbour. But when their stocks fall... it falls too. Faster. The result: it can move more in one day than an ordinary currency moves in a year. The ones holding huge amounts are called whales. <short pause> When the whale moves, it is the small fish that drown.

### lever

The most dangerous thing I found in this file. It's called leverage. You have a hundred dollars. The exchange lends you nine hundred. Now you're betting a thousand. The price goes up ten percent? You made a hundred. Your money doubled. <short pause> That is the bait. The price goes down ten percent? You lost a hundred. That is all of your money. And the exchange sells you out automatically to get its loan back. They call it: liquidation. Some exchanges offer a hundred times. So the price moves one percent against you... and you are done. On October 10th, 2025, the price dropped suddenly. In one day, over one and a half million traders were liquidated. Nineteen billion dollars, erased. Bitcoin had fallen less than fifteen percent. Most trading in this coin isn't real buying. It's leveraged bets. And the one who wins either way: the exchange. A fee when the price goes up, and a fee when it goes down. <short pause> Like a casino, sir. The house always wins. They found something that shakes on its own... <short pause> and bolted on a motor.

### who

So where are the twenty million that have been mined? Three to four million are lost. A million sit with the one who vanished. And more than a million are in Wall Street funds. And most humans who own some do not own a whole bitcoin. They own fractions. Pieces of a hundred-millionth. And the American government holds hundreds of thousands, seized from criminals. <short pause> So one of the biggest whales of the coin made to escape governments... is a government.

### suits

And the development nobody saw coming: the banks themselves moved in. In 2017, the head of the biggest money manager on the planet called bitcoin an index of money laundering. In 2024, the same company launched the biggest bitcoin fund in the world. What's a fund? You give your money to a company. It buys the bitcoin and holds it. And you only get the price. No key, no twelve words. And you pay a fee every year. So the humans who ran away from the middleman... paid a new middleman to save them the trouble of running. A coin built to make banks unnecessary. <short pause> And now the banks sell it to you. For a fee. Countries tried too. El Salvador made it legal tender in 2021. And backed off in 2025, to get a loan from the International Monetary Fund. America announced a strategic bitcoin reserve. Made of seized coins. It has not bought a single one. A company called Strategy more or less dropped its business to borrow and buy bitcoin. In 2026 it lost over eight billion dollars... in three months.

### debate

The humans are split on it. The critics say: it produces nothing. Its only value is that someone else will buy it from you for more. One of their most famous investors called it rat poison. The believers say: money nobody can print, and nobody can seize. Insurance against their own governments. A small test: in 2022, their prices caught fire. That was the moment the insurance was supposed to pay out. <short pause> That year, bitcoin fell more than sixty percent. Believers have rituals. The confident put laser eyes on their profile pictures. Those whose price fell say: I'm a long-term investor. <short pause> Meaning: I can't get out. I have recorded both views, sir, and I will not rule. One observation: both sides are extremely sure. <short pause> And on this planet... that has never been a good sign.

### score

Back to the three jobs of money, the ones I asked you to keep in the file from the first report. Pay with it? Rarely. Slow, fees change, and holders fear spending it... in case it's worth more tomorrow. Price things with it? No. Nobody prices bread in bitcoin. The price would change before the toast popped. Save it? Half a mark. Over the long run it has risen a lot. And it can lose half in a year. That's not savings, sir. That's a roller coaster. <short pause> By their own definition, it isn't money yet. It's a scarce asset... people bet on.

### dict

Two words from the Human Dictionary. HODL: a typo for hold, on a forum, in 2013. Now a creed: never sell. And FOMO: fear of missing out. <short pause> The number one reason humans buy at the top.

### recap

Summary of the report, sir. One: the value comes from scarcity... and belief. Two: the price is expectations, and leverage wipes people out. Three: by their own measure, it is not money yet. It is a bet. This report needs the warning most: nothing here is advice to buy or sell. <short pause> Put in money you need... and you'll usually lose it. Next report: the humans made the ledger run programs. Guarding without electricity, a digital dollar, and a bank with no bank. This has been Earth Report. Agent Peek. End of transmission.
