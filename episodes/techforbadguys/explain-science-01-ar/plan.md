# ملف العلم (The Science File): research and plan

Four reports, 7 to 10 minutes each, same format as the cryptocurrency file (`../HANDBOOK.md`). Researched on
2026-11-14. The owner's question: why should we believe science? Could they be fooling us? Why do studies keep
contradicting each other; does science lie?

**Peek's angle:** humans built a machine whose whole job is to doubt itself. It is the only club on the planet that
pays you for proving it wrong. That is why it is worth trusting, and also how you catch it when it fails.

**Line we hold:** honest, not preachy. Science gets it wrong, sometimes people cheat, and money bends things; we
show all of it, and we show who caught it (other scientists, journalists, replication). No medical advice, no
health claims for any food or drug, no religion-versus-science framing, no politics of one country. Where real
people were hurt (the vaccine fraud) the passage is `calm` and serious.

## Report 1: العلم يعني إيه؟ وليه نصدقه أصلاً؟ (what science is)

Hook: in 2011 a team of physicists announced particles faster than light, the thing Einstein says is impossible,
and asked the whole world to find their mistake. The culprit was a loose cable.

1. Science is a method, not a book of facts: say something that can be tested, test it, try to break it.
2. A stick and a shadow: Eratosthenes measures the planet (about 240 BC).
3. A claim must be able to fail ("my invisible dragon" kind of claims are not science).
4. The faster-than-light neutrinos (OPERA, 2011–2012): announced with a call to find the error; a loose
   fibre-optic cable and a clock; another experiment (ICARUS) measured light speed; the leader stepped down.
5. Why that story is the best advertisement for science: the error was found by science itself, in months.
6. Experiments, evidence, theory: what "theory" means to scientists (not a guess).
7. قاموس البشر: فرضية (hypothesis), نظرية (theory).
8. Recap; teaser: who checks a study before it reaches you?

## Report 2: البحث العلمي بيتعمل ويتراجع إزاي؟ (how a study is made and checked)

Hook: in 2015 a journalist made the news worldwide with a study saying chocolate helps you lose weight. He made it
on purpose, to show how easy it is.

1. What a paper is; about 3 million a year.
2. The fair test: control group, placebo, blind and double blind (drawn as a kitchen recipe, no medicine named).
3. Sample size: why 15 people prove almost nothing.
4. Measure many things in few people and something "significant" will turn up by luck (the chocolate hoax).
5. Peer review: who reviews, what they catch and what they miss; paid "journals" that accept anything.
6. Replication: a result counts when someone else gets it again.
7. How to read a headline: who, how many, compared to what, was it repeated.
8. قاموس البشر: عينة (sample), مراجعة الأقران (peer review).

## Report 3: ليه العلماء بيغيروا كلامهم كل شوية؟ (why scientists change their minds)

Hook: a doctor told his colleagues to wash their hands, deaths in his ward fell from about 18% to about 2%, and they
threw him out.

1. Changing your mind when the evidence changes is the feature, not the bug.
2. Semmelweis, Vienna 1847: handwashing, the drop, the rejection; accepted after germ theory.
3. Wegener, 1912: continents move; laughed at for decades; accepted in the 1960s with plate tectonics.
4. Barry Marshall drank a culture of bacteria (1984) to prove they cause stomach inflammation; Nobel 2005 with
   Robin Warren.
5. Eggs and cholesterol: the US guidelines dropped the daily limit in 2015; what that does and does not mean.
6. Correlation is not cause: ice cream and drowning both rise in summer.
7. One study versus what most teams find: the headline flips, the consensus moves slowly.
8. قاموس البشر: ارتباط (correlation), إجماع علمي (scientific consensus).

## Report 4: إمتى العلم بيضحك علينا فعلاً؟ وإزاي بنكشفه؟ (when science really fools us)

Hook: in 2023 more than ten thousand research papers were withdrawn, a record.

1. Yes, it happens: fraud, bias, money, pressure to publish.
2. Jan Hendrik Schön, Bell Labs: the young star of physics; same graph in different papers; misconduct in at least
   16 cases (2002), papers withdrawn from Science and the Physical Review journals.
3. The 1998 vaccine-autism paper: falsified; withdrawn by The Lancet in 2010; the author struck off the UK register.
   Serious passage: the fear it spread is still costing children. (`calm`, no medical advice.)
4. Money: the tobacco companies' 1969 memo, "doubt is our product": selling doubt instead of facts.
5. The replication crisis: 2015, 100 psychology studies repeated, about a third gave the same result.
6. Paper mills and fake peer review: one publisher withdrew more than 8,000 papers in 2023.
7. Who caught every one of these? Other scientists, journalists, replications. The machine works, slowly.
8. A checklist for the commanders (and the viewer): who says it, how many people, repeated or not, who paid, what
   most teams say.
9. قاموس البشر: سحب الورقة (retraction), تضارب المصالح (conflict of interest).
10. Sincere closing line: trust the method, not the person.
