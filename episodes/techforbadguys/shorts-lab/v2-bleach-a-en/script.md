# A toxic gas is being made in your bathroom. No. Not that one. #Shorts

**English (US) version of Studio A, version 2** (the Arabic Short is in `../v2-bleach-a/`). Topic: mixing chlorine
bleach with other bathroom cleaners. For the second, English channel. An adaptation, not a translation: the structure,
the safety content and the picture's choreography are the Arabic film's; the jokes, the cleaner's look and the home
were rewritten so an American viewer feels it was made for them.

**What the viewer can say after watching:** never mix bleach with another cleaner (toilet bowl cleaner, vinegar, glass
cleaner); rinse well between products and open a window; if it happens, get to fresh air at once, and with trouble
breathing call 911 and tell them what was mixed.

**Core message (one plain line, line 9, no joke in it):** "The rule: never mix bleach with any other cleaner. Ever."

## Facts and sources (unchanged from the Arabic version)

- Bleach mixed with an acid gives off chlorine gas; acid products include toilet bowl cleaners, vinegar and some glass
  and window cleaners. Bleach mixed with ammonia gives toxic chloramine gases; ammonia is in some glass and window
  cleaners. Symptoms: coughing, shortness of breath, watery and burning eyes, chest pain, nausea. (Washington State
  Department of Health: https://doh.wa.gov/community-and-environment/contaminants/bleach-mixing-dangers)
- If you inhale it, leave the area immediately and breathe fresh air; with trouble breathing or serious symptoms, get
  medical help. (US National Capital Poison Center: https://www.poison.org/articles/chlorine-gas)
- Rinse surfaces thoroughly before using a different product, keep good ventilation; if exposed, get fresh air, call
  emergency services and tell them which products were used. (London Fire Brigade:
  https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/safety/the-home/dangerous-chemicals/cleaning-products/)
- Line 5 says "toxic gas" for both gases (chlorine and chloramine) and "some glass cleaners", as the sources do.
- Lines 1-4, 7 (second half), 8, 11 and 12 are Peek's commentary, not facts. No brand, no treatment beyond fresh air
  and calling 911.
