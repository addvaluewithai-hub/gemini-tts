# مفيش ريحة غاز يبقى السخان أمان؟ غلط 🚿 #Shorts

**English title:** No gas smell means the water heater is safe? Wrong

**What the viewer can do after watching:** keep ventilation open whenever the gas water heater is on, have it installed by a specialised company and serviced regularly, stop using it if the flame is yellow or there is soot; and if headache, dizziness or nausea start, turn it off, get out to fresh air at once and call an ambulance.

Format: useful Short 02. The drawn scene fills the screen; Peek has three full-screen studio shots (lines 1, 6, 11).

   [PEEK: studio, full screen. He stands by a small closed window prop, sniffs the air once, writes "nothing" on his clipboard]

## Sources

- "Carbon monoxide gas is colourless and does not smell, so you cannot tell if it is around you"; symptoms include headache, dizziness, feeling sick, feeling weak, tiredness and confusion; appliances can produce it "if they are not installed properly, are faulty or are poorly maintained"; if you think you have it: stop using the appliance, go outside, get medical advice; prevention: appliances properly installed, serviced regularly by a qualified engineer, "keep rooms well ventilated, and make sure chimneys and flues are clean and well maintained" (NHS): https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carbon-monoxide-poisoning/
- "colourless, odourless, tasteless, poisonous gas produced by incomplete burning"; "can kill quickly without warning"; early symptoms "may easily be confused with food poisoning, viral infections, flu or simple tiredness"; warning signs: "yellow or orange rather than blue flames", "soot or yellow/brown staining around or on appliances" (UK Health and Safety Executive): https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/domestic/co.htm
- "Carbon monoxide – the silent killer"; "you cannot taste, see or smell it"; can occur if flues or vents are blocked; "Leave the property immediately. If someone is showing signs of poisoning or has collapsed, get them outside" and call an ambulance (London Fire Brigade): https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/safety/the-home/carbon-monoxide-safety/
- Egypt: periodic inspection and maintenance of appliances, and all installation work only through specialised gas companies; gas emergency line 129 (Egypt Gas, the state gas distributor): https://www.egyptgas.com.eg/StaticPages.aspx?Id=48
- **Left out, and why:** (1) "where the heater sits". I could not open any official source today that states a rule such as "the heater must be outside the bathroom" (Egyptian ministry and gas-holding pages were unreachable; only newspaper articles say it), so the script gives the three rules the official sources do support: ventilation, proper installation and servicing with a clear flue, and the flame/soot warning sign. If the manager has an official Egyptian source for the placement rule, I will add one line. (2) A carbon monoxide alarm: NHS and the Fire Brigade recommend one; left out for length, can replace line 5 if wanted. (3) Egyptian ambulance number: not found on an official page today, so «اتصل بالإسعاف». The 129 gas line is for gas leaks, so it is not in the narration. CDC's page was blocked (403).
- Lines 5, 6 and the first half of 11 are Peek's commentary. Line 5 dramatises the sourced fact that early symptoms are easily mistaken for flu or tiredness.
