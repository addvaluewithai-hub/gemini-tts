# ٣ حاجات من مصر القديمة لسه بتستعملها كل يوم 🏺 #Shorts

**English title:** 3 things from ancient Egypt you still use every day

Format: Countdown 3-2-1 (10 egypt3). Look: bold countdown cards. On screen: the number and one key word each.

## Narration

1. يا فندم، كل بشري على الكوكب ده بيستعمل كل يوم تلات حاجات بدأت عند المصريين القدام.. ومعظمهم مايعرفش.
   [Three bold face-down cards numbered 3, 2, 1 in front of a pyramid silhouette; Peek pointing at them]
2. تلاتة: <short pause> الورق.
   [Card 3 flips. Big «٣», key word «الورق»]
3. المصريين صنعوا ورق البردي من نبات على النيل، من أكتر من أربع تلاف وخمسمية سنة.
   [Papyrus reeds becoming a sheet with hieroglyphs]
4. وكلمة paper نفسها جاية من papyrus.
   [The word papyrus shrinking into the word paper on a modern notebook]
5. اتنين: <short pause> السنة.
   [Card 2 flips. Big «٢», key word «٣٦٥ يوم»]
6. تقويمهم كان تلتمية خمسة وستين يوم: اتناشر شهر، كل شهر تلاتين يوم.. وخمس أيام زيادة في الآخر.
   [A calendar wheel: 12 equal slices and 5 small extra days]
7. واحد: <short pause> الساعة.
   [Card 1 flips. Big «١», key word «٢٤ ساعة»]
8. المؤرخين بيقولوا إنهم أول حضارة قسمت النهار اتناشر جزء، والليل اتناشر جزء.
   [A T-shaped shadow clock in the sun with 12 marks; a night sky with 12 stars]
9. اتناشر واتناشر.. أربعة وعشرين.
   [12 + 12 = 24 on a modern phone clock]
10. يعني يا فندم، البشري اللي بيبص في ساعته وهو متأخر.. بيبص في فكرة عمرها أكتر من تلات تلاف سنة.
   [A human running late, checking a watch; behind it the ancient shadow clock]
11. والسؤال: <short pause> بعد كل ده.. ليه لسه بيتأخر؟
   [Peek deadpan beside the three open cards]
12. Peek.. انتهى التقرير يا فندم.
   [Sign-off card: EARTH REPORT, the bell]

## Sources

- Papyrus was first manufactured in Egypt as far back as the 3rd millennium BCE; the earliest archaeological evidence (Wadi al-Jarf) dates from c. 2560–2550 BCE, about 4,580 years ago; made from the papyrus plant, once abundant across the Nile Delta; "Papyrus is also the etymon of 'paper'": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus
- The ancient Egyptian civil calendar was a solar calendar with a 365-day year: twelve months of 30 days plus five extra (epagomenal) days: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_calendar
- "Most historians credit them with being the first civilization to divide the day into smaller parts"; as early as 1500 B.C. an Egyptian sundial divided sunrise-to-sunset into 12 parts; the night was also divided into 12; "Once both the light and dark hours were divided into 12 parts, the concept of a 24-hour day was in place": https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/experts-time-division-days-hours-minutes/
- **Changed from the brief:** the brief says "invented". The sources support "began with / first to", so line 1 says «بدأت عند» and line 8 says «المؤرخين بيقولوا». Papyrus is the ancestor and name-giver of paper, not modern paper (which came later from elsewhere), so line 3 says «ورق البردي». Honest limit on the hours: the Egyptians' hours changed length with the seasons; fixed equal hours came later, so the script claims only the division into 12 and 12.
- I picked these three because each could be sourced tonight; toothpaste, locks and similar popular claims are shakier and were left out. Britannica's pages were blocked (403).
