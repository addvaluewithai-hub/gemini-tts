# Network part 1, world picture: the shot list

Every board beat is one wordless illustrated shot (`video/sets/<board>.js`), drawn with `sets/kit.js` in the world of
the bank set (`sets/bank.js`: palette `P`, night town, houses, stars, skyline) and the network props in `sets/net.js`.
Host beats (`close`, `host`, `wide`) are unchanged. `host left/right` = Peek leans in from that side, so that lower
corner is kept empty (x > 1560 or x < 360, y > 480); the chapter chip sits bottom left. Timings are placed on the
words of the recording (`node sets/beats.cjs`).

Things that travel through the episode: **the blue phone** (the caller, in the blue house) and **the pink phone** (the
one who answers, in the pink house), **the tower** (lattice mast, three white panels, red light), **teal rings** (a
radio wave, always teal), **the cable** (dark under the ground, a gold pulse = the voice as numbers), **the core**
(a long building of blinking racks), **the SIM** (white card, gold chip), **hexagonal cells** in three colours
(pink, blue, gold groups), **the calendar page** (every date).

## voice (`voice`)

| step | what | why |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | The blue phone, big. Air rings (pale) hit its microphone; the mic turns them into a teal wave line that runs out of the phone across the frame. | voice → air shakes → microphone → electric wave |
| 1 | Camera pushes along the wave; gold dots drop on it at even steps, faster and faster; a counter rolls to 8,000 with a small "/ s" and a stopwatch of one second. | 8,000 measurements a second |
| 2 | Two waves: a calm speech wave sampled fine; a dense music wave with notes over it, sampled by the same few dots, comes out jagged and the notes crack. Host left. | music needs more; that is why hold music sounds awful |
| 3 | The dots lift off the wave as gold digits; the wave fades away under them. | each measurement becomes a number; the numbers travel |
| 4 | A tall pile of digits goes through a sieve: most fall away as dust, a small neat packet comes out. Host right. | compression: what reaches the other side is a summary of your voice |
| 5 | The packet slides back into the blue phone; teal rings leave the phone into the night air. Out through the rings. | the numbers leave the phone, into the air |

## route (`route`)

One long landscape traced left to right: the blue house with the blue phone at left, the near tower, the ground cut
away to show the cable, the core in the middle, the far tower, the pink house with the pink phone at right.

| step | what | why |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | The blue phone sends rings; they fade out after a short way (a dashed limit circle). | the wave does not go far |
| 1 | Camera tracks to the nearest tower; the rings reach it, its panels glow; a distance tick between them. | only to the nearest tower, a few hundred metres |
| 2 | The ground cuts away: a gold pulse runs down the mast into the cable underground and along to the core, whose racks blink. | from the tower down into a cable, to the core |
| 3 | The core sends the pulse on through another cable to the far tower near the pink house. | the core finds the other phone and sends it on |
| 4 | Close on the core: three panels light on its front — an ID card ✓, a map pin, a coin counter that ticks. The ID and pin flicker; the coin counter never stops. Host right. | it knows who, where, and bills; the billing never fails |
| 5 | From the far tower, rings to the pink phone, which rings. | the last few hundred metres are radio again |
| 6 | Pull back over the whole route: a pulse runs end to end while a stopwatch fills a quarter, the pink phone answers before the blue phone's rings stop. Host left. | the whole trip takes less than a quarter second |

## cells (`cells`)

| step | what | why |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | From above: a map of hexagons draws itself, a tower pops up in each. | the land is cut into cells, a tower in each |

| 2 | Camera pans: the city has many small cells, the desert one huge cell with one tower. Host right. | small cells in cities, large ones in the desert |
| 3 | One tower with three panels each facing its own way (three beams). A tiny weak phone beam beside a small bulb; the tower's panels grow big like ears. Host right. | antennas each facing a way; the phone is weak, the tower has big ears |
| 4 | The phone far from the tower: its rings grow bigger and its battery drains fast. Host left. | weak signal: the phone shouts, the battery dies |
| 5 | A neat street; a bare tower pops up and looks out of place. | cities do not want the look of towers |
| 6 | The tower dresses as a palm (fronds drop on it) and as a tree. | they disguise them as palms and trees |
| 7 | Close on the palm: the three white panels poke out of the fronds, the red light blinks. Host left. | a bad disguise |
| 8 | The blue phone lies still on a table; every few seconds a small "here" ring goes to the nearest tower. | the silent phone whispers "I am here" |
| 9 | Three towers around it; their rings meet at one spot and a pin drops there. Host right. | from signal strength at several towers the network finds it |
| 10 | A car drives across the cells; its call link hops from tower to tower as it passes the borders. | handover from tower to tower |
| 11 | The link breaks at a border: the line snaps, the phone screen shows a ? and small dashes. Host left. | when handover fails: hello? hello? |

## drop (`drop`)

| step | what | why |
|---|---|---|

| 5 | A phone with no SIM: an SOS key, rings go to any tower nearby and a ✓ comes back. Host right. | the emergency number works without a SIM, on any network |
