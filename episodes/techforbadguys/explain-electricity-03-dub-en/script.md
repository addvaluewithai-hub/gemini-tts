## Script

### intro

Sir, field report number three on the electricity file. Today: why the lights go out. Last time we learned the grid is a balance: what's made must equal what's used, every second. Today: what happens when the balance breaks. Strangest thing I found: in two thousand three, fifty million people in America and Canada sat in the dark. The cause: a tree.. and a frozen computer program. I'll tell you how a small fault grows, their biggest blackouts, the grid's enemies, and how a whole country climbs out of the dark.

### 1. DOMINO

This is a grid: stations, cities, and lines between them. The power is shared across all the lines. One line trips. For any reason. The load it carried doesn't vanish. It moves onto the lines next to it. They carry more than they can.. and trip too. And the next. And the next. In minutes, sometimes seconds, a whole region goes dark. Like workers carrying a heavy pole. One lets go.. the weight falls on the rest. The next one can't take it, and lets go too. And so on. The strange part: what trips these lines isn't the fault itself. It's the protection. Every line and station has a device that cuts it off before it burns. Each piece saves itself.. and the whole thing falls. Very human.

### 2. 2003

Back to the tree. August two thousand three, a hot day in Ohio. Hot wires stretch and sag. A high-voltage line sagged until it touched a tree nobody had trimmed. And tripped. Normally that's fixed in minutes. But the alarm software in the control room had a bug, and stopped without anyone noticing. So the engineers sat at screens saying: all fine. Within two hours the dominoes fell. Fifty million people in America and Canada, all of New York City among them. Some places waited two days for the lights. The subway stopped with people in the tunnels, and people walked home over the bridges. The losses ran into billions of dollars. Priciest tree on Earth.

### 3. 2012

Their biggest blackout ever was in India, July twenty twelve. The grid fell.. they brought it back.. and the next day it fell bigger. The cause: a hot summer, states drawing more than their share, lines carrying more than they could. On the second day, the dark covered an area with over six hundred million people. Trains stopped mid-track, and miners were trapped underground until they were pulled out. About half the country.. at once.

### 4. TEXAS

Not every blackout is a mistake. February twenty twenty-one, an ice storm hit Texas. Power plants and gas lines froze.. just as everyone switched on their heaters. The balance tipped. So the control room did the only thing left: it cut power by hand to over four million homes. In the depth of the cold. They said afterwards the grid was four minutes and thirty-seven seconds from total collapse. A collapse that could have taken weeks to repair. The state's official count: two hundred forty-six people died. Most from the cold. Electricity isn't a luxury for them, sir. It keeps them alive.

### 5. 2025

The last big one: twenty twenty-five, Spain and Portugal. The twenty-eighth of April, at noon, in fine weather. The voltage on the grid rose past its limit. Plants disconnected themselves to protect their equipment. So the voltage rose more. So more plants disconnected. In seconds, two whole countries without power. Tens of millions of people. Some places waited sixteen hours. The final report came out about a year later. It said: no single cause. A perfect storm of small ones. So after a year of investigation.. the suspect: circumstances.

### 6. ENEMIES

The grid has enemies from outside. First, the sun itself. In nineteen eighty-nine a solar storm hit the Earth, and Quebec's whole grid in Canada fell in ninety seconds. Millions sat in the dark for hours. In eighteen fifty-nine a far bigger solar storm came. It set off sparks in telegraph offices. Back then they had no power grids. If one like it came today.. nobody is sure what would happen. Second: humans themselves. December twenty fifteen, in Ukraine, people got into power companies' computers remotely and opened the switches one after another. About two hundred twenty-five thousand homes. The first known cyberattack to cut the lights. And third, seriously: the squirrel. A small animal climbs onto transformers and closes the circuit with its body. One of the most common causes of outages in North America. A storm-proof grid.. felled by a half-kilo animal. Who also comes off badly.

### 7. BLACK START

So the whole grid is down. How does it come back? Here's a neat problem: a power station needs electricity to start. Pumps, fans, computers. So they keep a few special stations, mostly dams, that can start on their own with nothing from outside. They start, wake the station next door, and keep growing the island piece by piece. Every piece must rejoin in exact step with the same pulse. That's why coming back takes hours, sometimes days. The fall takes seconds. The rise is slow.

### 8. WORDS

The human dictionary. Two words today. Blackout: a big outage. The whole grid goes down. And load shedding: cutting power in turns. Two hours off for one district, then the next, so the whole doesn't fall. In many countries that's not a dictionary word. It's a daily timetable.

### 9. RECAP

Report summary, sir. One: the grid falls like dominoes, and the protection that saves each piece.. is what spreads the fall. Two: the cause can be a tree, the cold, the sun, a hacker, or a squirrel. Three: the fall takes seconds, the rise is slow, from a few stations that start on their own. And a warning: when their lights go out, the danger isn't only the dark. In Texas people died from fumes of generators and heaters run indoors. Next report, the last in the file: where is electricity going? Batteries the size of warehouses, sunlight that became their cheapest power, and who on this planet is still waiting for a single bulb. This was Earth Report. Agent Peek. Transmission ends.
