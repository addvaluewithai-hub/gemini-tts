# ملف الكهربا (The electricity file): research and plan

Three reports, 7 to 10 minutes each, same format as the cryptocurrency file. Researched on 2026-11-12.
Every figure below is said in the script with its date or as an approximation. Facts under "General knowledge"
were not re-checked with a fresh search and are worded loosely in the script.

Safety line for the whole file: electricity can kill. Where the script touches danger it says so plainly and
describes nothing a viewer could try. No experiment is described as something to do.

## Report 1: الكهربا يعني إيه؟ (what electricity is)

Question: what is the thing in the wire, and why are there two kinds of it?

1. Charge: electrons, two kinds of charge, the word comes from the Greek for amber.
2. Static: charge that piles up and jumps; lightning is the same thing at scale.
3. Current: charge that moves round a closed loop; the electrons crawl but the lamp lights at once.
4. Volt, ampere, ohm, watt: the rooftop water tank analogy (pressure, flow, a narrow pipe).
5. Two kinds: direct current and alternating current.
6. The war of the currents: Edison, Tesla, Westinghouse.
7. Who won: alternating current runs the grid, direct current runs every device and the longest lines.
8. قاموس البشر: AC/DC, kilowatt-hour.

Sourced:
- Electron drift in a household copper wire is a fraction of a millimetre per second; the signal travels at a large
  fraction of the speed of light. https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/9-2-model-of-conduction-in-metals ,
  https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/electric/miccur.html
- Lightning: the US National Weather Service gives 100 million to 1 billion volts and 10,000 to 200,000 amperes;
  "about 300 million volts, about 30,000 amperes" is the usual typical value. About 44 flashes a second worldwide
  (other counts differ; said as "عشرات"). https://www.weather.gov/mlb/lightning_facts
- Pearl Street Station, New York: started 4 September 1882, direct current at 110 volts, about 400 lamps and
  roughly 80 customers on the first day; 508 customers and 10,164 lamps by 1884.
  https://ethw.org/Milestones:Pearl_Street_Station,_1882 , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Street_Station
- War of the currents: Edison's campaign against alternating current from 1888; Westinghouse lit the 1893 Chicago
  World's Fair. https://www.history.com/news/what-was-the-war-of-the-currents ,
  https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/edison-vs-westinghouse-a-shocking-rivalry-102146036/
- William Kemmler, 6 August 1890, first execution by electric chair; Harold Brown obtained Westinghouse
  alternating-current generators second-hand after Westinghouse refused to sell; Westinghouse paid for the appeal.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kemmler , Smithsonian link above.
- Niagara Falls (Adams plant) to Buffalo, 16 November 1896, alternating current at 11,000 volts over 20 to 26
  miles depending on the source (said as "أكتر من تلاتين كيلو"). https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-birthplace-of-the-ac-grid
- Changji to Guquan line, China: direct current at 1,100 kV, about 3,300 km, 12 GW, in service 2019 (records
  as claimed by State Grid). https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/state-grid-commissions-new-1100-kv-power-transmission-line-china.html

General knowledge: Thales and amber about 600 BC; Gilbert coined "electricus" in 1600; like charges repel;
Franklin's 1752 lightning proposal and Georg Richmann's death in 1753 repeating such an experiment; Volta's
battery 1800; the units are named after Volta, Ampère, Ohm and Watt; a static shock is thousands of volts with
almost no charge behind it; household sockets are 100 to 127 or 220 to 240 volts depending on the country; grid
frequency is 50 or 60 cycles a second; Tesla arrived in the US in 1884, worked briefly for Edison, and
Westinghouse licensed his patents in 1888; direct current at 110 volts could only serve about a mile round a
station; phones, computers, LEDs, solar panels and batteries are direct-current devices.

## Report 2: من المحطة للفيشة (how it is made and how it reaches a house)

Question: where does it come from, and how does it get to the socket in the same instant?

1. Faraday's trick: a magnet moving past a coil. A generator is a spinning magnet.
2. Who turns it: steam (coal, gas, nuclear), falling water, wind; solar panels are the exception.
3. The world's mix in 2025.
4. The journey: station, step-up transformer, pylons, step-down, street, house; why the voltage goes up.
5. The bird on the wire.
6. The balance: supply equals demand every second; frequency is the pulse.
7. One machine: a continent in step; the kettles of 1990, the slow clocks of 2018, Japan's two frequencies.
8. قاموس البشر: Hertz, Megawatt.

Sourced:
- World electricity in 2025 (Ember, Global Electricity Review 2026): coal 33.0%, gas 21.8%, hydro about 14%,
  nuclear 8.9%, solar 8.7%, wind 8.5%; renewables together 33.8%, ahead of coal for the first time in a century;
  demand 31,779 TWh. https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/global-electricity-review-2026/
- Losses between station and customer in the United States averaged about 5% in 2018 to 2022 (EIA).
  https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=105&t=3
- TV pickup: 2,800 MW in Britain at the end of the England v West Germany penalty shoot-out, 4 July 1990, still
  the record for a programme. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_pickup , https://www.drax.com/
- Continental Europe's clocks ran about six minutes slow by March 2018: 113 GWh missing from the Kosovo/Serbia
  area since mid-January, average frequency 49.996 Hz, about 25 countries.
  https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2018/03/07/591528390/a-political-dispute-puts-a-wrinkle-in-time-slowing-millions-of-european-clocks ,
  https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/european-clocks-six-minutes-late-serbia-kosovo-electricity-grid-spd
- Japan: 50 Hz in the east (German AEG generators, Tokyo, 1895), 60 Hz in the west (American General Electric,
  Osaka, 1896); in 2011 the converters between them carried about 1 GW, so the west could not cover the east's
  shortfall. https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2011/07/19/reference/japans-incompatible-power-grids/

General knowledge: Faraday's induction, 1831; most power stations boil water to turn a steam turbine, nuclear
included; Three Gorges Dam is the largest power station at 22.5 GW; generators produce at about 10 to 25 kV,
long lines run at hundreds of kV, streets at about 10 to 30 kV; heat lost in a wire grows with the square of the
current, so the same power at higher voltage loses less; transformers only work on alternating current; a bird
on one wire has no path through it; continental Europe is one synchronous grid from Portugal to Turkey serving
over 400 million people; operators plan for television schedules.

## Report 3: ليه النور بيقطع؟ (why it goes out and where it is going)

Question: how does one fault darken a country, and what is changing?

1. The domino: protection that saves the equipment spreads the failure.
2. 2003, North America: a tree, a software bug, 50 million people.
3. 2012, India: the largest ever.
4. 2021, Texas: four minutes from a collapse (serious passage).
5. 2025, Spain and Portugal: seconds.
6. Other enemies: the sun (Quebec 1989), hackers (Ukraine 2015), squirrels.
7. Starting from black.
8. Storage: batteries, and water pumped uphill.
9. Sun and wind: cheap, and at the wrong hour.
10. Those still in the dark, and the new appetite (data centres).
11. قاموس البشر: Blackout, Load shedding.

Sourced:

  into trees in Ohio; a race-condition bug silenced FirstEnergy's alarm system, so operators did not see the
  lines trip (US-Canada task force final report). https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2020-08/blackout-presentation.pdf ,
  https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/08/blackout_bug_report/ , https://practical.engineering/blog/2022/2/9/what-really-happened-during-the-2003-blackout
- India, 30 and 31 July 2012: two failures on consecutive days, over 600 million people in the affected area on the

- Texas, February 2021: more than 4 million customers cut off; the grid was 4 minutes 37 seconds from an
  uncontrolled collapse (ERCOT); the state's final count is 246 deaths.
  https://www.texastribune.org/2022/01/02/texas-winter-storm-final-death-toll-246/ ,

General knowledge: how a cascade spreads (a tripped line's load moves to its neighbours); power stations need
electricity to start and a few are kept able to start alone ("black start"); squirrels are among the commonest
causes of local outages in North America; pumped hydro stores energy by lifting water; solar output peaks at
midday and demand in the evening (the "duck curve"); load shedding is cutting some customers on purpose.
