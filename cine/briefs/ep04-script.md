# Episode 4, "Do heavy things fall faster?": script and shot plan (Gate 1, v2)

The one sentence: **no. Heavy and light things fall equally fast: the planet pulls harder on the heavy one, but the
heavy one is exactly that much harder to get moving. Only air makes a difference.**

Planned length about 89 s (80 to 90). Speech sets the timing, so the report times will come from the takes.

## What changed from v1 (the manager's critique)

1. **Cold open: an accident under threat, not a deliberate drop.**
   - The ball and the apple are already teetering on a plank at the top of the shelf, straight above Crowe. He patrols the aisle below with the plush under his arm, unaware.
   - Peek is frozen with both hands out, whispering his wrong idea: the heavy one lands first, then the apple, so two noises. The plank gives, and they fall level all the way down: ONE bang.
   - "for SCIENCE!" is gone. The dry science joke moves to Report A: "I ran an experiment. By accident."
   - The new title is WHICH LANDS FIRST?
2. **"Harder to get moving" is now felt.** Same push on two shopping carts: the empty one rolls away, the full one barely moves. That is one sentence in Report B and one board picture, before "It evens out".
   - Paid for by cutting Report A's recap of the drop, keeping only "Right above an agent".
   - "Forty times" is said three times in the whole report (it was six).
3. **The interruption has a threat.**
   - The announcement and the plush on every screen stay.
   - Then the TV wall switches to the security cameras. A camera on the rack swivels toward the shelf top, and for a beat one screen shows the top of Peek's hat from behind.
   - Peek shoves a pillow over the camera, and that screen goes to static just as Crowe turns to look at the security monitors behind his desk.
   - Crowe and Peek never share a shot.

Everything else is as in v1, including:
- the tag (the detector playback, "...Peek.");
- the Moon story and the facts;
- the decisions from your Gate 1 reply: the playback stays, about 89 s is fine, and the detector is big and lit with a waveform while it plays.

## The script

**Cold open:**
- Peek (take `drop`, a terrified whisper, 11 words): "Stay... stay... If it goes... heavy one first... then the apple..."
- After the bang: Peek's existing "Oh... no." (`audio/voice/p1-ohno`, cut as in Episode 1; no new take).

**Report A (Peek, normal report voice, slightly hushed, 62 words):**
> Commander. Field report number four.
> I ran an experiment. By accident. Right above an agent.
> Remember report one: the heavier the thing, the harder the pull. The ball is forty times heavier than the apple, so
> their planet pulls on it forty times harder.
> Theory: the harder pull wins. The ball lands first.
> Wrong. They landed together. One bang. Because the ball...

**Interruption, Crowe (take `crowe`, unchanged, first two lines):** "Attention, shoppers." / "Have you seen... this?"

**Report B (Peek, low, hushed, urgent, fully voiced, the Episode 2/3 Report B direction, 117 words):**
> ...is also forty times harder to get moving.
> Same push on two shopping carts: the empty one rolls away, the full one barely moves.
> More pull, but more stuff to move. It evens out. Everything speeds up the same: twenty-two miles an hour faster,
> every second.
> Except a feather. Air pushes back on everything, but on light, wide things, it wins.
> On their Moon there is almost no air. An astronaut dropped a hammer and a feather there. They landed together.
> So: more pull, but harder to move. Only air gets in the way.
> Which raises a question, Commander. Up there, astronauts bounce like bunnies. So why do you weigh less on the Moon?
> Investigating. Peek out.

**Tag (unchanged):**
- Crowe's detector plays back Peek's "...Peek out.", cut from the reportB take.
- Then Crowe (take `crowe`, last line): "...Peek."

**Totals:** 179 report words (v1 had 177, Episode 3 had 170). "Forty times" is said three times. The spoken numbers are still two: forty times, and 22 mph.

**How it sits beside Episode 1:** unchanged from v1.
- Report A quotes Report 1, says it is still true, and builds the wrong theory on it ("the harder pull wins").
- Report B shows why it does not: more pull, but more stuff to move. The carts let the viewer feel the second half before "It evens out".
- Wording I checked: never "gravity pulls on everything equally" and never "wants to fall". The wrong guess is labelled PEEK'S THEORY.

Physics: the plank is hinged at the shelf edge and swings down faster than things fall, so the ball and the apple are released together and really do land together. From 4.9 m, air makes a difference of under a hundredth of a second between them.

The service desk, medium, Crowe fully in frame with the plush. His wrist detector crackles, lit up with a moving
waveform, and Peek's voice comes out small and tinny: "...Peek out.", captioned like speech (your Gate 1 decision). Crowe slowly lifts the plush to his face: "...Peek." Now he has a
name. NEXT REPORT / WHY DO YOU WEIGH LESS ON THE MOON? lands over a dark band. Cut to black.

## Facts (two spoken numbers: forty times, 22 mph; unchanged from v1 except the carts)

| Fact | Value used | Source / check |
|---|---|---|
| Heaviest bowling ball | 16 lb (7.26 kg), the USBC maximum | USBC equipment specifications (16.00 lb maximum, no minimum), e.g. bowlingball.com BowlVersity |
| A medium apple | 182 g (0.40 lb) | USDA SNAP-Ed: 1 medium apple, 182 g |
| "Forty times heavier" | 7.26 / 0.182 = 39.9; said "forty times"; screen ×40 (no pounds spoken, so no kg needed) | computed |
| "Forty times harder" pull | the pull between the planet and a thing is proportional to the thing's mass, so ×40 | Newton's law of gravitation |
| "Forty times harder to get moving" | for the same push, the speeding-up is inversely proportional to mass | Newton's second law |
| Speeding up | g = 9.81 m/s² = 21.9 mph per second = 35.3 km/h per second; said "twenty-two miles an hour faster, every second" (as in Episode 1); screen 35 km/h per second | NIST standard gravity 9.80665 m/s²; computed |
| Cold-open drop | 4.9 m takes 1.0 s; air makes a difference of under 0.01 s between ball and apple (apple terminal speed about 37 m/s) | computed |
| Air on a feather | the drag is about the same size as its weight within a fraction of a second, so it drifts | standard physics |
| Moon air | the Moon has only an extremely thin exosphere; said "almost no air" | NASA (Moon facts; NSSDC hammer-feather page: "essentially in a vacuum") |
| Hammer and feather | Apollo 15, Commander David Scott, 2 Aug 1971, at the end of the last moonwalk. He dropped a 1.32 kg aluminium geologic hammer and a falcon feather from about 1.6 m at the same moment; they reached the ground together. | NASA NSSDC "The Apollo 15 Hammer-Feather Drop" (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo_15_feather_drop.html); NASA Science "The Apollo 15 Hammer-Feather Drop" |
| What I do not say | The feather's mass (NASA's own page says the 0.03 kg in the 1971 report is probably a typo), the year, and the astronaut's name. On screen: APOLLO 15 only. | NSSDC page |
| Shopping carts | the same push gives a 40 times heavier load one fortieth of the speeding-up; the picture is qualitative (the empty cart rolls off the board, the full one creeps) | Newton's second law |
| Astronauts bounce like bunnies | Apollo moonwalkers often moved in a two-footed "bunny hop" | NASA Apollo surface footage and lunar surface journal descriptions |
