## The script

**Cold open (Peek, take `throws`):** "Back!" / "Get BACK!" / "GET! BACK!"  **(take `oops`):** "...Oops."

**Report A (Peek, normal report voice, 84 words):**
> Commander. Field report number two.
> Their Moon is not bolted to the sky. I checked. And here, everything falls. So: it is coming down.
> I threw apples at it. The harder I threw, the farther they landed.
> But their planet is round. Throw hard enough, and the ground curves away under the apple as fast as it falls.
> It never lands. It falls all the way around... and hits you in the back of the head.
> So why doesn't the Moon fall? Because it...

**Interruption:** Crowe (take `crowe`, first half): "Thrown... from up here."  Commander over the radio (take
`commander`): "Because it WHAT, Agent Peek?"

**Report B (Peek, hushed stage whisper, 86 words):**
> ...is falling. All the time.
> But it is also moving sideways. Two thousand three hundred miles an hour.
> Without the pull, it would fly off in a straight line. With it, it falls a dime's thickness every second... just
> enough to bend its path into a circle around us. Once every twenty-seven days.
> So: the Moon is falling. It moves sideways so fast that it keeps missing us.
> Which raises a question. The same pull reaches their astronauts. So why do they float?
> Investigating. Peek out.

**Tag:** Crowe (take `crowe`, second half): "Small mouth... big teeth."

Total report: 170 words for about 62 s (Puck about 2.7 words a second after tightening; tempo 1.0, no speed-up).

"Back!" The apple arcs up toward the Moon, tops out, and drops back a few steps in front of him: bonk, it rolls. |
| 2.0 to 3.9 | Medium: he grabs a second apple from a paper bag, bigger wind-up, "Get BACK!". The camera follows the arc across the alley; it lands on the roof opposite with a thunk. |
| 3.9 to 6.0 | Close: he glares at the Moon, takes one furious bite (crunch, chew, deadpan), then a full spin wind-up, "GET! BACK!". Whip pan after the apple, far out over the city. |
| 6.0 to 7.0 | Long-lens, across the street: the apple smashes a lit window (flash of glass) and drops to the pavement. |
| 7.0 to 8.6 | Street level: Crowe's black silhouette under a streetlamp. His detector's light flips and beeps fast; the bitten apple rolls to his shoe; he picks it up and his head tilts up toward the roofs. |

The three throws are the lesson: same angle, harder each time, lands farther each time. Report A redraws them.

## The interruption (about 7 s, on the real rooftop; the explanation stops mid-sentence)

| Time | Shot |
|---|---|
| 0.0 to 1.2 | The stairwell door slams open: hard cut from the board to the rooftop wide. Light spills from the doorway. Peek freezes, eyes huge, then dives behind the AC unit; his hat lags in the air and he snatches it. |
| 1.2 to 3.2 | Crowe steps out, detector beeping, torch beam sweeping. He raises the bitten apple into his torch: "Thrown... from up here." |
| 3.2 to 5.2 | Close on Peek behind the AC: his antennae light up and the Commander crackles out loud: "Because it WHAT, Agent Peek?" Peek clamps both antennae down with his hands, cheeks puffed. Wide: Crowe's head turns, the beam swings to the AC unit, the beeping speeds up. |
| 5.2 to 7.0 | The beam crawls over the top of the AC, just above his hat brim... and moves on. Crowe walks to the far parapet to look down at the street. Peek, pressed flat, whispers: "...is falling." and we are back on the board. |

It pushes the story (Crowe is on the same roof, holding the evidence) and re-hooks with the half-finished answer. The
report continues in a changed state: whispering, hiding, the antennae dimmed, and the threat stays in the frame.

## Tag (about 5 s; no separate end card)

Crowe turns from the parapet, kneels at the AC unit where Peek hid (faint pink footprints lead away), and holds the
bitten apple up to his torch: two big front-tooth marks in a tiny bite. "Small mouth... big teeth." The next question
lands on the same shot: NEXT REPORT / WHY DO ASTRONAUTS FLOAT? Cut to black. (He now knows the size of what he is hunting.)

## Facts, with sources (three numbers: 2,300 mph, a dime per second, 27 days)

| Fact | Value used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Moon's mean orbital speed | 1.022 km/s = 2,286 mph, said "two thousand three hundred miles an hour", screen 2,300 mph / 3,700 km/h | NASA NSSDC Moon Fact Sheet, https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html |
| Moon's distance | 384,400 km (semimajor axis), used only in the calculation below, not spoken | same |
| One orbit | 27.32 days (sidereal), said "twenty-seven days" | same |
| How far it falls each second | v^2 / 2r = 1.022^2 / (2 x 384,400 km) = 1.36 mm (1.35 mm from GM / 2r^2); screen 1.4 mm | computed from the NASA values above |
| A US dime is 1.35 mm thick | "a dime's thickness" | US Mint coin specifications, https://www.usmint.gov/learn/coins-and-medals/circulating-coins/coin-specifications |
| Newton's cannonball (throw hard enough and it falls around the Earth, no air) | the thought experiment | Newton, A Treatise of the System of the World (1728), the mountain and projectile passage; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton%27s_cannonball |
| Astronauts are still pulled (about 90 percent of surface gravity at the station's height) | "The same pull reaches their astronauts", no number | my own check: (6371 / (6371 + 420))^2 = 0.88; widely quoted as about 90 percent |

Physics checks I applied to the pictures: the three cold-open throws share one angle and only the speed changes (one
thing at a time); the cannonball paths are integrated with inverse-square gravity, not hand-drawn; the pull arrow
always points at Earth's centre and the sideways arrow is at right angles to it; the 1.4 mm gap is labelled not to
scale; "Peek's theory" is labelled as his wrong idea.
