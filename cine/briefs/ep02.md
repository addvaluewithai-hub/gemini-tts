## Episode 2: what is decided
- **Question:** Why doesn't the Moon fall? (Episode 1 ended on it.)
- **The one sentence a viewer must be able to repeat:** the Moon *is* falling; it moves sideways so fast that it keeps
  missing the Earth.
- **Length: about 90 seconds (85 to 95).** Episode 1 was 65 s. This is a deliberate experiment agreed with the owner:
  the two retention curves will decide the length of later episodes. Do not make it shorter or longer.
- **One Crowe interruption in the middle of the report** (about 6 to 8 s, roughly halfway): the report comes under
  threat, Peek reacts, and the report continues in a changed state (hiding, whispering, moving). It must push the
  story forward and re-hook the viewer; it must not feel like a pause. The explanation picks up exactly where it
  stopped, so write the split on a cliffhanger sentence ("So why doesn't it fall? Because it ... " / interruption /
  the answer).
- **Cold open (idea approved by the owner):** Peek is terrified of the huge Moon hanging over the city. He throws an
  apple at it, harder each time. It always comes down, farther away each time. A window breaks. Crowe's detector
  reacts. About 10 s; **frame one must already be the hook** (no establishing shot, no slow start).
  The throws are also the lesson: throw harder and it lands farther; throw hard enough and it never lands. Use them
  again in the report (Newton's cannonball). That link is the reason this cold open was chosen.
- **Continuity:** Peek wears his hat with the PRESS card from the first frame (he found it at the end of Episode 1;
  it is his disguise). The coat is his own. Crowe stays a black silhouette with a long coat (`N.crowe`). At the end of
  Episode 1 Crowe found the capsule and asked "What... is this?"; he is now investigating.
- **Tag (about 4 to 5 s):** Crowe one step closer, with the next question over the picture: NEXT REPORT / WHY DO
  ASTRONAUTS FLOAT? No separate end card.
- **Voices** (Vertex AI Voice Factory): Peek = `Puck`, the Commander = `Charon` (radio-filtered in the sound script),
  Crowe = `Algenib`. Copy the style prompts from `cine/audio/ep01/*.json`.

Facts to check yourself with sources before you write a number (do not trust this list): the Moon's distance (about
384,400 km, 239,000 miles), its speed (about 1 km/s, 2,300 mph), one orbit in about 27 days, how far it falls toward
the Earth each second (about 1.4 mm). Peek speaks miles; the screen adds km. Pick at most three numbers.

What is yours to design: the script, the shots, the board pictures, the interruption, the tag, the sounds.

## House rules (learned on Episode 1)
1. **Value first.** One question, an answer the viewer can see, one or two facts worth repeating, a one-line summary.
   Every picture must be physically right; check each one as a physicist would (Episode 1 had two wrong pictures in
   its first cut: unequal arrows for a mutual pull, and a comparison that changed two things at once).
2. **Peek's voice:** dry, deadpan, a little smug, a tiny detective. Short sentences. He is wrong about Earth because
   of how things are on his planet, and the jokes are played straight.
3. **Write to length; do not speed speech up to fit.** Puck speaks about 2.7 words a second after silences are
   tightened. Episode 1's report was 149 words for 50 s at tempo 1.11, which is the upper limit. For about 62 s of
   report in two parts, write about 160 to 170 words. Record part A and part B as separate takes.
4. **Shorts safe zones:** keep faces, captions and titles out of the bottom 480 px and the right 190 px of the
   1080 x 1920 frame. Board captions sit at y 1100; cinematic captions at y 1340.
5. **Captions:** Anton, at most 3 words and 22 characters a line, the spoken word in yellow (`X.captions`). The
   speech-to-text mishears names ("Peak" for Peek, "mi" for miles): fix them in the caption replacements in the sound
   script, as Episode 1 does.
6. **Sound:** effects stay quiet. Rain is very low outdoors and **silent under the report**. The mix is -14 LUFS. All
   effects are synthesized in the sound script (numpy, scipy); no downloaded audio.
7. **No dead air on screen:** never an empty board for more than half a second; every board scene has motion; Peek
   under the board acts (gestures, real lip sync from `audio/mouths.cjs`).
8. **Speech drives time.** The sound script finds each sentence's beat from the word timestamps and writes the cue
   file; the shot reads the cue file. Never hard-code report timings in the shot.
