# Episode 3, "Why do astronauts float?": script and shot plan (Gate 1)

The one sentence: **astronauts float because they are falling, all the time, together with their ship; gravity is
still pulling on them almost as hard as on the ground.**

Planned length about 88 s (80 to 90). Report times come from the takes (speech drives time).

## The script

**Cold open:** Peek (take `float`): "I... I FLOAT!"  Then Peek's existing "Uh-oh." (`audio/voice/p4-uhoh`, no new take).

**Report A (Peek, normal report voice, 74 words):**
> Commander. Field report number three.
> Good news: I floated. Bad news: for two seconds.
> Theory: the higher you go, the weaker their pull. Up where their astronauts live, it is switched off. Zero gravity.
> I checked. Wrong. Their station is only two hundred and fifty miles up. And the pull there is about nine tenths of
> the pull down here. Almost the same.
> So why do they float? Here is the secret. They are...

**Interruption, Crowe (take `crowe`, first three lines):** "Not you." / "Not you." / "...You."

**Report B (Peek, hushed through a frozen grin, fully voiced, 96 words):**
> ...falling. All the time.
> On that ride, my seat fell with me. Nothing pushed on me. So I floated.
> Their station is a ride that never stops. It falls toward the planet, but it moves sideways seventeen thousand miles
> an hour, so it keeps missing. Just like their Moon, only lower and faster.
> So: astronauts are not out of the pull. They float because they fall around the planet, together with their ship.
> Which raises a question, Commander. Up there, a wrench floats right beside a pencil. So do heavy things fall faster?
> Investigating. Peek out.

**Tag, Crowe (take `crowe`, last line):** "These teeth... are sewn on."

Report total: 170 words (Episode 2 was 171 for 62.5 s). "Zero gravity" is said only as Peek's theory, labelled
PEEK'S THEORY on the board, and corrected in the next sentence.

Physics: in free fall the rider and the seat fall together, so he only drifts a little off the seat (the release jolt
gives him a small upward push relative to it); he does not fly away. The hat drifts with him and is held for the
2 s by being close to his head; I do not show it falling at the same rate as him later (drag would lift a hat).

## The interruption (about 8.5 s; new, not a hide-behind-a-box beat)

| Time | Shot |
|---|---|
| 0.0 to 1.2 | The detector's beeping, close. Peek snaps into a plush pose on the shelf: stiff arms out, a fixed grin, eyes front. |
| 1.2 to 3.0 | Wide on the stall: Crowe (rig) walks along the counter and settles in front of the shelf. He raises the bitten apple (handLFront) beside the first plush and compares its teeth with the bite: "Not you." |
| 3.0 to 4.8 | He takes a half step (walk + settle) to the next plush: "Not you." The next one along is Peek. |
| 4.8 to 6.2 | Close on Peek (no Crowe in the shot, only the warm torch light reaching in from the side): he sucks his big teeth in behind closed lips, cheeks puffed, a bead of sweat. |
| 6.2 to 8.5 | Wide: Crowe skips past Peek to the plush beside him, whose stitched teeth are bigger: "...You." He tucks it under his arm, turns and walks away, satisfied. Peek exhales: "...falling." and we are back on the board. |

It pushes the story (Crowe uses the bite from Episode 2, the clue "small mouth, big teeth"), it is a new kind of gag
(hiding in plain sight, not behind an object), and it re-hooks on "They are...". **Rule 2 needs your call:** Peek is
in disguise here, so Crowe is close to him in the wide shot by design (that is the joke: he looks straight at him and
takes the wrong toy). I keep the rest of rule 2: Crowe's beam never crosses Peek's face, Crowe is never in Peek's
close-up, and at Crowe's closest he is beside the next plush, not at Peek. If you want rule 2 literally, the fallback
is: Peek hides inside a claw machine's glass case among the toys while Crowe walks the midway outside it, 350 px away.

## Facts (three numbers: 250 miles, nine tenths, 17,000 mph)

| Fact | Value used | Source / check |
|---|---|---|
| Station height | about 400 to 420 km = 249 to 261 mi, said "two hundred and fifty miles", screen 250 mi / 400 km | NASA ISS facts (orbits at about 250 mi); Wikipedia ISS |
| Gravity there | (6371 / 6771)^2 = 0.885 at 400 km, 0.880 at 420 km; said "about nine tenths", screen about 90 % | computed (inverse-square law); quoted by NASA as about 90 % |
| Speed | sqrt(GM / r) = 7.66 to 7.67 km/s = 17,140 to 17,160 mph = 27,600 km/h; said "seventeen thousand miles an hour" | computed; Wikipedia ISS 7.66 km/s; NASA rounds to 17,500 mph |
| One lap | 92.4 to 92.8 min, 15.5 laps a day | computed; not used (number budget) |
| Free fall feels weightless | rider and seat fall together, no contact force; gravity still acts | standard physics; drop-tower explainers (Tomtit science park, IOP Physics Education) |
| A wrench and a pencil float together in orbit | everything in the station is in the same free fall | standard physics (equivalence of falling) |

Words checked against the brief: never "no gravity" as fact, never "feel the pull"; "zero gravity" is Peek's theory
and is corrected; "Nothing pushed on me" (no contact force), not "nothing pulled".
