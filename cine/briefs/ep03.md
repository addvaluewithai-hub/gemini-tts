Same roles as Episode 2: you build, the manager (`session_01Ey97nkx4NcbTjgRgQHKwU1`) reviews and speaks for the owner.
Episode 2 is published and the owner loved it; its length and its mid-report story beat are now the format. Read

## What is decided
- **Question:** Why do astronauts float? (Episode 2 ended on it: "The same pull reaches their astronauts. So why do
  they float?")
- **The one sentence a viewer must be able to repeat:** astronauts float because they are falling, all the time,
  together with their ship; gravity is still pulling on them almost as hard as on the ground.
- **The link to Episode 2 is the lesson:** the station does exactly what the Moon does, only much lower and much
  faster. It falls around the Earth and keeps missing. Use the same pictures (the pull arrow to the Earth's centre, the
  sideways arrow, the circle) so a viewer of Episode 2 recognises them at once.
- **Why falling feels like floating:** when you and your floor fall together, nothing presses on you. Show it with
  one thing a viewer has felt (a lift that starts down, the top of a drop ride, a jump) before the station.
- **Length: 80 to 90 seconds.** Same structure as Episode 2: cold open about 10 to 12 s, report part A, one story
  interruption of 6 to 9 s about halfway, report part B, tag about 5 s with the next question.
- **Cold open (idea approved by the owner):** Peek hides on a drop-tower ride at a night fair. At the top the ride
  lets go and for two seconds he floats off his seat: "I float!" (in Episode 1 he said "On MY planet... we float", so
  he thinks he has it back). Crowe is waiting at the bottom with his detector. Frame one must already be the hook: a
  readable face with a clear emotion, no establishing shot.
- **The interruption must not repeat Episode 2's** (hiding behind a box while a torch passes). Find a new one that
  fits a fair, pushes the story (Crowe one step closer: he now has the bitten apple and knows his quarry is small) and
  re-hooks on a half-finished sentence. For example, Peek frozen among the plush prizes of a stall while Crowe looks
  along the shelf. Propose your best idea at Gate 1.
- **Tag:** Crowe one step closer, with NEXT REPORT / DO HEAVY THINGS FALL FASTER? over the picture.
- **Crowe is on the rig:** `lib/crowe.js`, the black silhouette, default look. He walks with `walk` from the distance
  covered and `settle` at every start and stop; he never slides and never freezes mid-stride. `handLFront` when he
  holds something across his chest. Nothing of the old `N.crowe` in new shots.
- **Voices:** Peek = `Puck`, the Commander = `Charon`, Crowe = `Algenib`, style prompts as in `audio/ep02/*.json`.
  Report B in Episode 2 used the hushed, fully voiced direction; the owner liked it.

Facts to check yourself with sources before any number goes in (do not trust this list): the station's height (about
400 km, 250 miles), its speed (about 7.7 km/s, 17,000 and some mph), one lap in about 90 minutes and about 16 laps a
day, gravity at that height (about 90 % of the ground's). Peek speaks miles; the screen adds km. At most three numbers.

Be exact with words: astronauts are not "weightless because there is no gravity" and they do not "feel the pull"; they
are in free fall. Never write "zero gravity" as a fact; if Peek says it, it is his mistake and the report corrects it.

## Rules added by the Episode 2 reviews (all of them cost a round; do not repeat them)
1. Frame one: a face, turned toward what it fears or wants, with an unmistakable emotion.
2. Hidden means hidden: if a character hides from another, put a solid thing between them and real distance (at least
   350 px of empty set in the wide shot); a searching beam never crosses the hider's face; no searcher in the
   hider's close-up.
3. A speaker is fully in frame while speaking; pan after the line. Captions clear at a cut to a shot of something
   else.
4. Board pictures fill the board; labels at least 56 px; nothing in the bottom 60 px of the board; no label that
   repeats the caption; a label appears when its thing happens, never as a faded ghost before.
5. When a picture compares two sizes, draw both at one scale with matching brackets, and say what is not to scale.
6. When Peek's situation changes, his body under the board changes too (crouch, side-glances, smaller gestures).
7. A prop that carries the meaning of a line is big enough to read on a phone (at least 400 px wide in its close-up).
8. Nobody slides. Anything that moves through a shot moves on a rig, or the move happens inside a cut.
9. Marks of the wrong idea: when Peek's theory is wrong, label it as his (PEEK'S THEORY) before it is corrected.
