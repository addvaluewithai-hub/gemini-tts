# Brief: Episode 4, "Do heavy things fall faster?"

Same roles and the same way of working as Episode 3 (`briefs/ep03.md`; read it again, and `briefs/ep02.md` for the
series, the pipeline and the limits). Episode 3 took two review rounds and the owner's verdict was "very good". This
brief only says what is new.

## What is decided
- **Question:** Do heavy things fall faster? (Episode 3 ended on it: "Up there, a wrench floats right beside a pencil.
  So do heavy things fall faster?")
- **The one sentence a viewer must be able to repeat:** no: heavy and light things fall equally fast; the planet does
  pull harder on the heavy one, but the heavy one is exactly that much harder to get moving. Only air makes a
  difference.
- **It must be squared with Episode 1**, where Peek reported "the heavier the thing, the harder it pulls". That is
  still true, and it is what makes the question a real one: a harder pull ought to mean a faster fall. Say so, then
  show why it does not (twice the pull on twice the stuff gives the same speeding-up). A viewer of Episode 1 should
  feel the series is building, not contradicting itself. Episode 1's number is free to reuse: every second of
  falling adds 22 mph, and now the viewer learns that this is true for everything.
- **The air:** a feather is slow because the air pushes back, and that matters most for light, wide things. Take the
  air away and it falls like a hammer. The best true story for this is the hammer and the feather dropped on the Moon
  by an Apollo astronaut; check every detail of it against a NASA source before you use it.
- **Length: 80 to 90 seconds**, the same structure: cold open about 10 to 12 s, report part A, one story interruption
  of 6 to 9 s about halfway, report part B, tag of 5 to 7 s with the next question.
- **Cold open (idea approved by the owner):** a bowling ball and an apple hang above Agent Crowe's head; they come
  down and land together. How they come to hang there, where this is, and what they land on are yours to design.
  Crowe is the butt of the joke, not a casualty: a cartoon near miss or a hat flattened, nothing that reads as real
  harm. Frame one is already the hook, with a readable face and a clear emotion.
- **A new place.** Not the rooftop and not the fair. Somewhere that gives you things to drop.
- **A new mid-report beat.** Not hiding behind a thing (Episode 2) and not a lookalike line-up (Episode 3). It pushes
  the story and re-hooks on a half-finished sentence.
- **Where the chase stands:** Crowe has the bitten apple, knows "small mouth, big teeth", and now holds a plush toy
  that looks like his quarry: pink, antennae, buck teeth. He has a likeness. The tag takes him one step closer again.
- **Tag teaser:** NEXT REPORT / WHY DO YOU WEIGH LESS ON THE MOON? (Episode 5's approved cold open is in a museum at
  night, among astronaut mannequins; do not use the museum now.)
- **Crowe** only on the rig (`lib/crowe.js`), black, `walk` with `settle`. **Voices** as before; Report B in the
  hushed, fully voiced direction if his situation calls for it.

Be exact with words. Do not say gravity pulls on everything equally: it pulls harder on heavier things. What is equal
is how fast they speed up. Do not say heavy things "want" to fall faster. Mark Peek's wrong guess as PEEK'S THEORY.
At most three numbers; Peek speaks miles and pounds, the screen adds km and kg.

12. Everything in Episode 3's rules 1 to 9 still applies.

## How we work
Exactly as on Episode 3: Gate 1 (script, shot plan, fact sheet with sources, voice request files with job ids
`peek-en-ep04-<name>`), then stop and wait; the manager brings the owner's word and records the takes; build against
a placeholder meanwhile; Gate 2 on the real takes with your own critique, then stop and wait; the manager renders and
delivers. The commit goes in the first line of every report. Voice budget: 10 takes. New files: `shots/ep04.js`,
`audio/ep04_sound.py`, `audio/ep04/`, a new set file. Episodes 1 to 3 must render exactly as before. Keep working on
branch `peek-en-ep02`; merge the base (`origin/ccr-087a9bfa-62989o`) first.

harder to move, and only air gets in the way", that sits comfortably beside Episode 1, with a new place, a new
